Markers vs crayons for toddler coloring books

Hop Along Books · 2026-10-08

The most common one-star review of children's coloring books is some version of "the marker bled through and ruined the next picture." Here's what is going on and what to do about it.

Why it happens

Print-on-demand paperbacks (which is most coloring books on Amazon now) use fairly thin paper. Markers, especially washable kids' markers, soak through. Crayons and colored pencils sit on top of the paper and don't.

Crayons: the best choice for 3-5

Thick crayons are easiest for small hands to grip, they don't bleed, and a 3-year-old can press hard without tearing the page. For a first color by number book, crayons win.

Colored pencils: good for 4-5

Finer control, no bleed, but they need a bit more hand strength. Nice for the harder levels.

Markers: fun, but check the book first

Kids love markers. If yours insists, pick a book where pictures are printed on one side only, so bleed-through lands on a blank or activity page instead of the next picture. Slip a sheet of scrap paper behind the page as well.

What to look for in a book

  • One picture per page, one-sided printing
  • Something useful on the back of each picture (so the page isn't wasted)
  • Thick, clear outlines that are easy to see even if a marker spreads a little
